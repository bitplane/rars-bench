Teaching RAR 1.4 to repeat itself

RAR 1.4 is the oldest format rars writes and it was the worst served. The match finder was finding repeats the format had no cheap way to spell, so it spelled them expensively. Three things went in. Lazy matching, so a match is only taken when the next position cannot do better. Near-distance LongLZ, where a distance of 256 or less carries a plus-eight bias on the length field, making eleven bytes the shortest thing it can encode. And the four old-distance codes, which re-use a recent distance in a couple of bits instead of coding it again. Near candidates search on their own budget, so looking for them does not starve the far-match search that was already there.

  • Every class improved and none regressed. The byte-weighted total went +7.11% to +2.20% and the median cell +10.46% to +2.69%. The worst cell is now bin-shell at +3.80%, where the worst before was logs at +19.81%.
  • Logs moved furthest, from worst to best. +19.81% to +1.44%, which is -15.34% off the packed size. Repeated log lines are near repeats of each other, so they are exactly what the old-distance codes and the near-distance LongLZ were missing.
  • PNG is the control and it barely moved: +2.26% to +2.10%. Already-compressed bytes have no near repeats to find, so a better match finder has nothing to work with. A large move there would have meant a bug, not a win.
  • It landed in three steps. Headers went 46,154 bytes to 45,133 with near-distance LongLZ, 44,590 with the old-distance codes, then 41,976 once lazy matching tied them together. Most of the win came last, because the first two gave the parse cheaper things to choose between and the third is what chooses.
  • One win was handed back on purpose. DOS rar 1.402 does not reliably decode an old-distance match whose length symbol is all ones. The format permits it and the encoder refuses to emit it, on all four codes, not just the one that also reserves that value as a buffer toggle. Staying readable by the binary being imitated is the whole point of writing RAR 1.4.
  • Small tier only. rar 1.402 is a DOS binary driven through DOSBox on a 30 second wall-clock limit, and it cannot encode the 4 MiB corpus inside it. Those rows would carry a real-looking size that is really a truncated file, so RAR 1.4 is measured on the 10 small-tier classes and left out of the timing chart entirely.

Pricing the PPMd escapes

PPMd models text well enough that a repeated span costs a fraction of a bit per byte. An LZ escape token inside that stream costs six model symbols, coded through contexts where the escape byte is rare, and the bytes it copies never reach the model, so the literals after it are predicted from the token's own bytes. The tokeniser was taking every match of 32 bytes or longer regardless. It now measures what a literal is costing right now, straight off the range coder, and emits a token only when the literals it replaces cost more. The model parameters did not change: order 8, 25 MB and escape 2 are what WinRAR 3.00 declares in its own streams, and were already what we wrote. Only which bytes get escaped moved.

  • Nothing got bigger. Ten of the 16 cells at the top level moved and all ten moved down. The binary classes did not move at all, which is the control working: PPMd is only tried on text, so a change inside it cannot reach an ELF.
  • Near parity is the result, not a lead. Against WinRAR 3.00 at m5 the text classes went from losses of one to five percent down to a fifth of a percent: headers +4.55% to +0.21%, docs +2.33% to +0.18%, manpages +1.45% to +0.12%. Only mixed crossed into a win. Logs moved furthest and is still the worst of them at +1.60%.
  • The win is concentrated in short, repetitive text. The log corpus is -9.16% smaller, the 4 MiB prose member only -0.32%. Repeated log lines are exactly where a marginal match looks attractive and is not; running prose has fewer of them to get wrong.
  • The cost model has not finished the job. On dpkg.log a forced PPMd stream still comes back at 2,911 bytes where coding every byte as a literal gives 2,065, so the escapes there are still over-emitted and a second trial is what rescues the member. The trial is a backstop, not the fix, and the gap between those two numbers is what is left to win.
  • The timings are measured outside the harness. The sweep ran while the laptop was building something else and reported 40.4 s to store files the quiet sweep stored in 0.3 s, so its wall clock is off the page. The table above alternates the two binaries over the same members, best of three, so a busy patch hits both equally. Packed sizes are deterministic and were never in question.
  • Read the two 4 MiB rows and the ELF, and ignore the rest of that table. The small classes finish in under a tenth of a second, which is mostly process startup, so their ratios are noise. Prose costs 1.31x and the source tree 1.42x, which is the second PPMd pass the backstop adds plus two range-coder readings per symbol. The stripped ELF is the control at 1.00x for byte-identical output: nothing was spent where PPMd never ran.

What the tree parse bought

The change before this one, kept because it is what made level 5 usable at all. Level 5 prices every position in a block, which means searching at every position. Doing that through a hash chain was the whole cost. Rebuilding the search as a binary tree, and searching once per block rather than once per pricing pass, left the packed sizes almost exactly where they were and took the time apart.
